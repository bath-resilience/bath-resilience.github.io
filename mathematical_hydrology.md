- **Date:** 2-3 December 2026
- **Location:** Claverton Down Rooms, University of Bath

## Summary

The workshop will provide a forum for mathematicians and hydrological modellers to explore in depth how theoretical analysis can provide insights about the behaviour, calibration and robustness of rainfall-runoff models.

The motivation for the workshop includes recognition that there is a vast array of hydrological models in the literature, many of which are related variants of common physical and conceptual structures. As part of this workshop, we will probe the question of whether the mathematical basis of physics-based modelling approaches can be compared with the broad category of conceptual models. The focus will be on how mathematics can help to simplify and understand these different models, rather than on "solving" predictive equations or implementing models.

Through analysis, we want to share and develop approaches to compare and assess models based on their mathematical structure, in ways that can reduce or remove the confounding effects of data errors when models are calibrated and tested against observations. We will discuss the scaling of model responses with respect to changes in baseline hydrological conditions and forcings from extreme events or gradual change.

We are hoping that this workshop can form a unique gathering of not just theoretical modellers and applied mathematicians, but will also bring significant engagement with hydrological modellers who are interested in examining the potential for mathematical analysis to help with issues including:

- Model comparisons and benchmarking
- Understanding the robustness and limits of predictions in extrapolation
- Model considerations in conjunction with climate change
- Process representations at different scales

## Outcomes

The outcomes of the workshop will be:

- A network of contacts between hydrological modellers, model users and mathematicians, enhancing communities of practice.
- An overview of hydrological modelling challenges and relevant analytical techniques with potential to help advance science and practice in hydrology.
- Seed ideas that may be developed further into proposals.
